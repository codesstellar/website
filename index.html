Ask an engineer
Is post-quantum cryptography actually necessary yet?

The threat is not that a quantum computer breaks your traffic tomorrow. It is that encrypted traffic captured today can be stored cheaply and decrypted once one exists. If your data has to stay confidential for ten years or more, the migration decision is already live. The NSA has set January 2027 as the point at which new national security systems must support CNSA 2.0 algorithms.

Do we have to replace our whole stack?

No. Most migrations are hybrid: classical and post-quantum algorithms run side by side so that nothing breaks if one is later found weak. We normally start with key exchange on the most sensitive channel, then signatures, then storage. The written scope sets out that order for your systems specifically.

How do you price work?

Fixed fee against a written scope, milestone based. Long running platform work moves to a monthly retainer once the initial build is handed over. We do not bill by the hour, because it rewards the wrong thing.

Who owns the code and the models?

You do, on handover, including source, infrastructure definitions and documentation. Where we bring an existing product such as Q-Vault or Q-AmChain, that is licensed to you on terms agreed before the build starts, and we say so plainly in the scope.

Can you work with our existing engineering team?

Yes, and it usually produces a better result. We can lead the build with your team embedded, or take a defined component while your team owns the rest. Handover is smoother when your engineers have been in the repository the whole time.

What does a first conversation involve?

Forty five minutes with an engineer, not a salesperson. Bring the problem, any constraints you already know about, and whatever you have tried. You will leave with a point of view on the approach whether or not you work with us.
